# pii-recall-guard

Automatic PII tagging for data catalogs with a statistical guarantee on the one number that matters to a privacy officer: the share of personal-data columns that slip through untagged.

## Executive summary

Every modern data platform copies columns from operational systems into lakes, warehouses and feature stores faster than any governance team can review them. Privacy regulation (LGPD, GDPR and their peers) does not care how many columns were tagged correctly. It cares about the ones that were not: an untagged column with customer emails ends up in a shared analytics schema, a vendor extract or a model training set, and from that point the exposure is no longer theoretical.

Most column scanners report accuracy. Accuracy is the wrong contract. A scanner can be 98.7% accurate, as the classifier in this repository is, and still miss 15% of the most dangerous column type, free-text comments where some customers typed their phone or email. What a risk owner actually needs is a dial: "miss at most 5% of PII columns", with a defensible statement of why that bound holds and what it costs in false quarantines.

This project builds that dial. A cheap value-based classifier tags each sampled column, a conformal layer measures how unsure it is, ambiguous columns are escalated by reading more rows, and a conformal risk control step picks the quarantine threshold that bounds the miss rate. The headline results, all measured on synthetic data and reported without selection:

* At a 5% target, the two-stage guard misses 4.3% of PII columns on a held-out test set while quarantining only 0.2% of non-PII columns. Over 300 random re-splits, the average miss rate is 4.97%, on target.
* Escalating only 4.2% of columns to a larger sample beats a stronger model: on the escalated slice, an LLM reading the same rows missed 3 of 5 PII columns, while the guard missed 1. All three LLM misses were columns whose sample contained no leaked value at all.
* The guarantee holds on average, not in every run: in about 44% of re-splits the realised miss rate lands slightly above 5%. A high-probability variant (Clopper-Pearson, delta 0.1) brings that down to 11% at a small cost in recall headroom.
* The guarantee breaks under distribution shift. When leaks in production free text become rarer than in calibration data, the miss rate jumps to 11.8% against a 5% target. Calibration data must be refreshed from the population being guarded.

For a platform team, the practical value is a scanner whose threshold is set by the risk appetite of the business, not by a data scientist's intuition, plus a measured view of which column types need more rows, more human review or both.

## What it does

```
sampled column values (no column names)
        |
        v
[1] featurize: hashed char n-grams of values and of value shapes, plus 14 distribution stats
        |
        v
[2] softmax regression over 19 classes (9 PII, 10 non-PII)
        |
        v
[3] Mondrian conformal set at 95% per class
        |
        +-- set mixes PII and non-PII classes? --> [4] escalate: re-read the column with 4x rows (min 60)
        |                                                  |
        v                                                  v
[5] P(PII) = probability mass on PII classes  <-------------
        |
        v
[6] quarantine if P(PII) >= lambda_hat, with lambda_hat chosen by conformal risk control
```

Column names are deliberately not used. Real schemas are full of `col_17`, `attr_x` and misleading names, so the detector works from values alone.

## Technical deep dive

### Data

`piiguard/synth.py` generates columns as a scanner would see them: 5 to 25 sampled rows, 30% of columns with nulls and junk tokens (`N/A`, `null`, a lone dash), and per-column format choices. The generator contains collisions on purpose, because that is what makes production data hard: surnames and first names that are also city names (Lima, Santos, Florence), birth dates whose years overlap generic business dates, raw 11-digit national ids that look like long numeric order ids, and free-text comment columns where only 8% to 35% of rows leak an email or phone. Card numbers pass Luhn and national ids pass a CPF-style mod 11 check so that shapes are realistic; every value is synthetic.

Each column carries its own seed, and `Column.resample(n)` re-reads the same population with more rows, keeping the original rows as a prefix. That is what makes escalation a faithful model of "query more rows of the same table".

Splits (seed 20261101, all balanced across 19 classes): 1140 training columns, 760 for the conformal sets, 950 for risk control, 1900 for test, plus a shifted test set of 1900.

### Classifier

Multinomial logistic regression in NumPy (`piiguard/model.py`), full-batch Adam with L2. The features are the mean, over non-empty rows, of hashed character 1 to 3-grams of the lowercased value and of its shape (`Ana-42` becomes `Aaa-99`), plus statistics such as blank rate, digit share, share of rows matching an email or phone pattern, and the mean and spread of parsed years. The model is intentionally simple: conformal guarantees hold for any score function, and a transparent model makes the failure modes legible.

### Prediction sets

With the LAC score s(x, y) = 1 - p_y(x), split conformal takes the ceil((n+1)(1-alpha))/n empirical quantile q of calibration scores and returns C(x) = {y : 1 - p_y(x) <= q}. Under exchangeability, P(y in C(x)) >= 1 - alpha marginally. Mondrian conformal computes one quantile per true class and upgrades the guarantee to every class separately, which is the only version that protects the rare, hard class.

| alpha | variant | coverage | mean set size | coverage of free_text_pii | worst class |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | marginal | 0.913 | 0.92 | 0.74 | free_text 0.55 |
| 0.10 | Mondrian | 0.930 | 0.94 | 0.93 | city 0.76 |
| 0.05 | marginal | 0.957 | 0.96 | 0.79 | free_text_pii 0.79 |
| 0.05 | Mondrian | 0.968 | 1.02 | 0.98 | city 0.87 |
| 0.02 | marginal | 0.984 | 0.99 | 0.84 | free_text_pii 0.84 |
| 0.02 | Mondrian | 1.000 | 19.00 | 1.00 | degenerate |

Two honest observations. First, the marginal guarantee is met while the class we care most about sits at 74% to 84%: marginal coverage is paid for by easy classes. Second, Mondrian at alpha 0.02 collapses to the full label set, because with 40 calibration columns per class the required rank ceil(41 x 0.98) = 41 exceeds n. The rule of thumb is n_k >= 1/alpha - 1 per class, so a 98% class-conditional guarantee needs at least 49 calibration columns of every class. The worst-class Mondrian numbers (city at 0.76 and 0.87) are the minimum over 19 classes of a finite test sample with 100 columns each; the guarantee is in expectation over calibration draws, and one draw can land below it.

LAC also produces empty sets (8.4% at alpha 0.1): when q is small, a column whose probability is spread over two or three classes has no class with p_y >= 1 - q. Downstream, an empty set is treated as "not ambiguous" and the column goes straight to the risk-control gate.

### Escalation

A column is escalated when its Mondrian set at 95% contains both a PII and a non-PII label. On the test set this happens for 4.2% of columns, all of them free text (72 clean, 8 leaking). Of the 100 leaking free-text test columns, 89% show a leak in the original sample (16 rows on average) and 100% do after re-reading at least 60 rows. No model, however large, can tag a leak that is not in the rows it was shown.

### Conformal risk control for the quarantine decision

The governance decision is binary: quarantine when P(PII) >= lambda. On the n PII columns of the risk calibration set, the loss L_i(lambda) = 1{P(PII)_i < lambda} is bounded by 1 and non-decreasing in lambda. Conformal risk control picks

lambda_hat = sup { lambda : n/(n+1) R_n(lambda) + 1/(n+1) <= alpha }

and guarantees E[FNR] <= alpha on new PII columns. The escalation rule depends on the Mondrian thresholds, so those are fitted on a separate calibration set: if the same data chose the rule and calibrated the risk, exchangeability between calibration and future columns would be broken.

Single held-out test set:

| target alpha | stage | lambda_hat | miss rate (FNR) | false quarantine (FPR) | clean free text quarantined |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | one stage | 0.9973 | 0.076 | 0.000 | 0.00 |
| 0.10 | two stage | 0.9984 | 0.082 | 0.000 | 0.00 |
| 0.05 | one stage | 0.9232 | 0.037 | 0.002 | 0.01 |
| 0.05 | two stage | 0.9781 | 0.043 | 0.002 | 0.01 |
| 0.02 | one stage | 0.3207 | 0.019 | 0.008 | 0.03 |
| 0.02 | two stage | 0.8277 | 0.024 | 0.003 | 0.01 |

Escalation does not lower the miss rate (CRC spends the full budget either way); it lowers the price. At the strict 2% target, one stage must drop lambda to 0.32 and quarantines 3% of clean free text, while two stages keep lambda at 0.83 and quarantine 1%. At looser targets both are near zero false quarantines and the difference is noise.

300 random re-splits of risk-calibration plus test columns (model and escalation rule fixed):

| alpha | variant | mean FNR | p90 FNR | runs with FNR > alpha | mean FPR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.10 | two stage, CRC | 0.101 | 0.122 | 51% | 0.0004 |
| 0.10 | two stage, UCB delta 0.1 | 0.083 | 0.107 | 16% | 0.0006 |
| 0.05 | two stage, CRC | 0.050 | 0.066 | 44% | 0.0016 |
| 0.05 | two stage, UCB delta 0.1 | 0.037 | 0.051 | 11% | 0.0025 |
| 0.02 | two stage, CRC | 0.020 | 0.032 | 41% | 0.0041 |
| 0.02 | two stage, UCB delta 0.1 | 0.011 | 0.021 | 11% | 0.0086 |

CRC is exactly what it promises: the average miss rate sits on alpha, and almost half of individual deployments land above it. If the contract is "at most 5% with 90% confidence", use the UCB variant (`conformal.ucb_threshold`): it scans lambda upward and stops at the first point where the exact Clopper-Pearson upper bound on FNR exceeds alpha, which is fixed-sequence testing and needs no multiplicity correction. The empirical exceedance of 11% to 16% is above the nominal 10% partly because each run's test FNR is itself a noisy estimate of the true FNR on about 900 PII columns.

### Distribution shift

The shifted test set keeps every class identical except that leaking free text now leaks in 2% to 8% of rows instead of 8% to 35%. With lambda_hat calibrated on the old population at alpha 0.05, the miss rate rises to 11.8% and recall on leaking free text falls to 18%. Two things fail at once: exchangeability, so the guarantee no longer applies, and the features themselves, because a mean-pooled "share of rows with an email" is diluted by rare leaks. A max-pooled "any row with an email" indicator would be more robust, and the calibration set should be drawn from recent production samples, not from a historical labelled corpus.

### LLM second opinion

A Claude model (claude-sonnet-5-5, recorded in `eval/llm_labels.json`) labelled 100 test columns zero-shot from values only, blind to names, labels and classifier output (`eval/llm_prompt.md`). The batch is 50 escalated and 50 non-escalated columns.

| slice | columns | PII columns | LLM class accuracy | classifier accuracy | LLM miss rate | guard miss rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all | 100 | 31 | 0.97 | 0.94 | 3 of 31 | 2 of 31 |
| escalated | 50 | 5 | 0.94 | 0.90 | 3 of 5 | 1 of 5 |
| not escalated | 50 | 26 | 1.00 | 0.98 | 0 of 26 | 1 of 26 |

The LLM is the better classifier on the rows it sees, with zero false PII calls. Its three misses are all leaking free-text columns whose sample contains no leak: the information was not in the input. The guard reads more rows exactly where the conformal set says it should, and that is worth more than a stronger model on the same rows. Sample sizes here are small (5 PII columns in the escalated slice), so treat this as a mechanism, not a benchmark.

## Limitations

* Synthetic data. Collisions were designed in, but real catalogs have long tails (multilingual names, partial masking, JSON blobs) that this generator does not cover.
* Balanced classes. In real catalogs PII columns are a minority; FNR is conditional on PII, so the guarantee is unaffected, but FPR in absolute terms scales with the non-PII volume.
* The guarantee is about columns exchangeable with the calibration set. New source systems, new locales or new leak behaviour require new calibration data.
* Escalation assumes reading more rows is cheap. For very wide or very cold tables it is not, and the escalation budget becomes a cost trade-off of its own.
